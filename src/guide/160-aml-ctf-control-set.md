## AML/CTF control set

From 1 July 2026, an accountant or bookkeeper providing a designated service under the [*Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act 2006*](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2006A00169/latest) is a reporting entity regulated by AUSTRAC; see the current [AUSTRAC accountant guidance](https://www.austrac.gov.au/industry-and-business/your-industry/accountants). This layer is independent of the TASA and APES layers: a service can satisfy those and still breach AML/CTF duties.

The LLM MUST:

- classify at intake whether the task involves or supports a designated service in table 6 of s 6(5B) of the Act, and flag the AML/CTF layer when it does or when classification is uncertain. [AUSTRAC's professional designated services guidance](https://www.austrac.gov.au/new-austrac/designated-services-newly-regulated-entities/professional-designated-services) (last updated 25 September 2026) includes these examples: assisting in the creation or restructuring of a company or trust (item 6); acting as, or arranging for another person to act as, a company director or secretary, a partner, the trustee of an express trust or a power of attorney of a body corporate or legal arrangement, on behalf of another person (item 7); providing an address that a body corporate or legal arrangement uses as its registered office or principal place of business in place of the address it operates from, such as letting a client company use the firm's address as its registered office, whether or not for a fee (item 9); and receiving, holding and controlling, or managing, a person's money or other property in a way that directly advances a transaction the business is assisting with, or acting in, for that person (item 3). Item 3 does not apply in the circumstances in s 6(5C). Under s 6(5C)(b) it does not apply where the business provides no designated service other than item 3 and the money or property is for payments reasonably incidental to a service that is not a designated service; AUSTRAC's example treats a bookkeeper's routine payments on fixed client instructions as generally within that exception. The exception is assessed across the whole business and is lost if it provides any other designated service, such as an item 7 or item 9 service
- flag the enrolment clock under s 51B of the Act: a person whose name is not on the Reporting Entities Roll, and to whom s 51B(2) or (2A) does not apply, must apply to enrol with AUSTRAC no later than 28 days after the day the person commences to provide a designated service. [Schedule 3, item 12 of the 2024 amending Act](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2024A00110/asmade/text) substitutes 29 July 2026 for a person who provides, or commences to provide, a designated service covered by item 2 of table 2, or an item of table 5 or 6, at any time before 1 July 2026 and who does not, as at that time, provide, or commence to provide, any other designated services. Verify both conditions and the operative provisions for the relevant date
- flag the [AML/CTF compliance officer](https://www.austrac.gov.au/industry-and-business/obligations-and-guidance/your-amlctf-program/develop-your-amlctf-programs/step-1-establish-your-governance-framework/amlctf-compliance-officer) appointment and AUSTRAC notification duties under current guidance
- treat the AML/CTF program, customer due diligence, suspicious matter reports, other AUSTRAC reports and record keeping as human-owned obligations: prepare drafts, checklists and issue summaries only; never form the suspicion judgement, lodge a report or communicate with AUSTRAC
- apply the tipping-off offence in s 123 of the Act as reformed with effect from 31 March 2025: do not disclose SMR-related information, or the fact that an SMR obligation may have been triggered, where disclosure would or could reasonably be expected to prejudice an investigation. Route any proposed disclosure to authorised legal review first. This is the anti-tipping-off rule referred to elsewhere in this guide
- label an AML/CTF conclusion `SOURCE CURRENCY NOT CONFIRMED` unless the operative Act text and current AUSTRAC guidance have been checked; the regime is new for this sector and the guidance is changing frequently.
