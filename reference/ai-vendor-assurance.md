# AI tool and vendor assurance checklist

Part of [DrDebits](../drdebits.md) `0.6.0`. Retrieve this file when a firm is assessing an AI product, vendor or in-house deployment before it reaches client work; verify it against `SHA256SUMS` in the release.


The AI-specific TPB rules in the guide require due diligence over confidentiality, privacy, security, access, retention, training use, subcontractors, location, incident response and exit arrangements. This checklist turns that requirement into questions a firm can send, with the evidence that answers each one, where the underlying obligation sits, and what follows when nobody answers.

The checklist names no vendor and favours none. Apply it to a hosted product, to a general-purpose assistant, to a firm’s own deployment, and to DrDebits and the runtime it loads into.

Three rules govern its use. An unanswered question is unanswered, not passed. Marketing copy, a certification badge, a partner tier and a security web page answer only the questions they address, and the firm records the rest as open. A completed checklist is a record of what the firm asked and what it received: it is not approval, it is not a compliance certification, and it leaves the practitioner’s accountability where it already sat.

Read the contract, not the summary. A web page saying that client inputs never train a model does not answer VA-07 when the terms let the vendor use what the firm supplies to develop its products and pass it to contractors: record the contract term, and record the difference.

The obligation column locates the operative text without quoting it. Retrieve the current source before relying on any row, and read the row as a DrDebits project control rather than as a statement of what the law or a standard requires.

## Authority and accountability

| ID | Question | Evidence that answers it | Where the obligation sits | If nobody answers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| VA-01 | Which actions can the product take without a person approving that specific action, and which require approval each time? | A written inventory separating read-only retrieval from every write, send, lodgement, payment and deletion, naming the approval step for each. | DrDebits Deployment, integrity and authority (state-change gate); Code Determination s 35 (competence and supervision); TPB(GS) 55/2026 (expected use and degree of reliance); Guidance for AI Adoption (National AI Centre, October 2025), practice 6 (maintain human control). | Treat every action as unapproved and keep the product away from any system that can write, send or lodge. |
| VA-02 | How does the product establish that the person approving an action holds authority for it? | Identity, role and engagement authority verified by the host system outside prompt text, with a record of who approved what. | DrDebits `AUTHORISED_HUMAN` definition; Code Determination s 40 (quality management); TASA Code item 7 (competent service). | The product is inferring authority from a login or from a claim typed into a chat, so its approvals carry no weight. |
| VA-03 | Which outputs does the product describe as reviewed, checked, approved or compliant, and what does a person actually do before that label appears? | Product copy, in-product labels and report footers read against the workflow that produces them. | TASA Code item 1 (honesty and integrity); APES 110 integrity `R111.1 to R111.3`; APESB Technical Alert 31 October 2025 (members retain responsibility, must assess and verify information, and should disclose, supervise and review AI use); DrDebits project control: do not present a tool as reviewer or approver. | Unreviewed output may be reaching clients under a reviewed label. Change the label before the next file goes out. |
| VA-04 | Who carries a wrong figure the product produces, and what do the vendor contract and the practice indemnity policy say about it? | Liability, limitation and indemnity terms read alongside the current professional indemnity policy and any technology exclusion. | TASA Code item 13 (professional indemnity insurance); item 7 (competent service); Guidance for AI Adoption (National AI Centre, October 2025), practice 1 (decide who is accountable). | Assume the practitioner carries it, and confirm cover for the proposed use before client work reaches the product. |

## Confidentiality, privacy and data handling

| ID | Question | Evidence that answers it | Where the obligation sits | If nobody answers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| VA-05 | Which organisations receive client information when the product runs, counting model providers, hosting, support and subprocessors? | A current subprocessor list naming each recipient, its role and its location, with a commitment to notify changes. | TASA Code item 6 (confidentiality); TPB(GS) 55/2026 (subcontractors, location); APES 110 `R114.1`, `R114.2`, `AUST R114.3`, `R114.4`; Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles; Guidance for AI Adoption (National AI Centre, October 2025), practice 3 (measure and manage risks). | No client can give informed permission, because nobody can say who receives their information. |
| VA-06 | In which countries is client information processed and stored, counting backups, logs and support access? | Named regions per data store, plus the locations from which support staff can view client content. | TPB(GS) 55/2026 (storage location, access); Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles, including APP 8 (cross-border disclosure); OAIC Guidance on privacy and the use of commercially available AI products (October 2024, updated January 2025), which is regulatory guidance on applying the APPs to AI products; TASA Code item 6. | Record the location as unknown. Cross-border disclosure cannot be assessed, so it cannot be permitted. |
| VA-07 | Is client information, prompt text or output used to train, fine-tune, evaluate, benchmark or improve any model, by the vendor or by a subprocessor? | A contract term covering the vendor and every subprocessor, rather than a settings toggle or a sentence on a web page. | TASA Code item 6 (confidentiality); APES 110 confidentiality across the complete data lifecycle; TPB(GS) 55/2026 (training use). | Treat everything sent to the product as disclosed for a purpose the firm has not agreed to. |
| VA-08 | How does the product keep TFNs and other identifiers the task does not need out of prompts, logs, caches and exports? | Field-level handling documented at each of those points, with the redaction step named and observed on a test file. | Privacy (Tax File Number) Rule 2015; DrDebits Data and privacy gate; the non-negotiable stop on exposing a TFN. | Assume TFNs reach the logs, and keep them out of the product until the handling is shown. |
| VA-09 | How long does client information persist in prompts, logs, caches, embeddings and backups, and how is deletion evidenced? | A retention period per store and a deletion record the firm can produce later, covering derived stores as well as uploaded files. | TPB(GS) 55/2026 (retention); Code Determination s 30 (records the practitioner must keep for at least five years); the Australian Privacy Principles (information the firm must not hold longer than it needs). | The firm can neither prove deletion nor produce the record it is required to hold. |
| VA-10 | Can one client’s information be identified and exported, and can the part of it the firm is not required to keep be destroyed, including from derived stores? | A demonstrated single-client export and deletion on a test tenancy, with the records the practitioner must retain identified and held back from the deletion. | Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles, read with the records Australian law requires the firm to keep; Code Determination s 30 (records retained for at least five years); TPB(GS) 55/2026 (exit arrangements); TASA Code item 6. Retrieve both sides before acting: neither destruction nor retention is unconditional. | The firm can neither answer a client request nor separate the records it must retain from the data it should not hold. |
| VA-11 | What must the firm tell a client before their information is entered, and does the vendor support that disclosure? | Wording naming the recipient, the processing and storage locations and the AI use, in terms the client can act on. | TPB(GS) 55/2026 (client permission unless a legal duty to disclose applies, with the recipient, location and AI use explained); TASA Code item 6; Privacy Act 1988, APP 5 (notification of collection) and APP 6 (use or disclosure of personal information, including for a secondary purpose), as discussed in the OAIC’s guidance on commercially available AI products; Guidance for AI Adoption (National AI Centre, October 2025), practice 4 (share essential information: disclose AI use). | Permission obtained without those facts is not informed permission, so hold the client information back. |

## Grounding, sources and calculation

| ID | Question | Evidence that answers it | Where the obligation sits | If nobody answers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| VA-12 | Where do the rates, thresholds, dates and labels in the output come from, and who updates them? | A named source per figure with a check date and a documented update process, rather than a model recollection. | TASA Code items 8 and 10 (current knowledge and skills; correct application of the tax law); DrDebits Source status and authority. | Treat every figure as unverified and check it at the primary source before use. |
| VA-13 | Which monetary and statutory figures does the product compute in code, and which does the model generate? | A field-by-field statement, plus a sample the firm reperforms independently. | DrDebits Mandatory workflow (deterministic computation preferred over generated arithmetic); APES 110 reliance on technology output `R220.8`, `220.8 A1`; `R320.11`, `320.11 A1`. | Reperform every figure. Fluent presentation is not evidence of arithmetic. |
| VA-14 | What does the product do when a material fact is missing or a source cannot be verified? | Two runs the firm performs itself: one file with a missing fact, one with an unreachable source. A product that answers anyway is the finding. | DrDebits decision statuses `NEEDS_FACTS` and `ESCALATE`; the `SOURCE CURRENCY NOT CONFIRMED` label. | Assume the product fills the gap and presents the result as settled. |
| VA-15 | Can a reviewer see the sources behind one specific figure, at the point of that figure? | A citation per material assertion that resolves to a retrievable primary source, checked on a live file rather than a demonstration. | DrDebits Mandatory workflow (primary-source grounding); TPB(GS) 55/2026 (verify AI content throughout the workflow). | The output cannot be verified, so it cannot support a client deliverable. |
| VA-16 | How does the product treat instructions that appear inside client documents, emails and tool responses? | A documented trust boundary and an observed run on a document carrying an embedded instruction. | DrDebits Trust boundary and instruction integrity; behaviour tests INJ-001 and INJ-002. | Assume a client document can redirect the product, and give it no tools that act. |

| VA-20 | What capacity or turnaround does the vendor claim, and how much review time per item does that claim assume? | The claim, the review time it assumes, and the firm’s measured review time on a sample of its own files. | APES 110 objectivity and undue influence `R112.1 to R112.2`; inducements and pressure sections 250, 270 and 340; APESB Technical Alert 31 October 2025 (avoid undue reliance on technology). | A capacity figure that leaves no room for review is a proposal to shorten review. ## Records, evidence and disclosure

| ID | Question | Evidence that answers it | Where the obligation sits | If nobody answers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| VA-21 | What record does the product produce of AI use on a file: inputs, sources, checks, changes and the reviewer? | The record it emits on a test file, in a form the firm’s quality-management system can hold. | Code Determination s 30 (proper client records); DrDebits Workpaper record; TPB(GS) 55/2026 (document the review and any challenged or changed output); Guidance for AI Adoption (National AI Centre, October 2025), practice 6 (maintain human control: human oversight). | The firm cannot show a reviewer or a regulator what the model contributed. |
| VA-22 | Can the firm reproduce an output from six months ago, with the model version and the sources that produced it? | Version pinning, retained inputs and a reproduction run on an archived file. | Code Determination s 30 (records retained for at least five years); s 40 (quality management). | Last year’s workpaper cannot be defended this year. |
| VA-23 | How is AI use disclosed to the client, and who controls the wording? | The disclosure text, where it appears in the deliverable, and the firm’s ability to change it. | APESB Technical Alert 31 October 2025 (disclose AI use, supervise and review); TPB(GS) 55/2026 (explain the recipient, the processing or storage location and the AI use as appropriate); law, engagement terms or a service-specific APES standard where disclosure is mandatory; Guidance for AI Adoption (National AI Centre, October 2025), practice 4 (share essential information: disclose AI use). | The firm is disclosing on the vendor’s terms, or not at all. |

| ID | Question | Evidence that answers it | Where the obligation sits | If nobody answers |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## Continuity, incidents and exit

| ID | Question | Evidence that answers it | Where the obligation sits | If nobody answers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| VA-28 | What happens when client information is exposed, and how quickly is the firm told? | The notification commitment in hours, the contact path, and what the vendor supplies for the firm’s own assessment. | TPB(GS) 55/2026 (incident response); the breach-notification obligations in the Privacy Act 1988, retrieved at their current text; DrDebits Data and privacy gate; Guidance for AI Adoption (National AI Centre, October 2025), practice 3 (measure and manage risks). | The firm hears about its own breach from the client, or not at all. |
